# David Baker — Verified Quotes

All quotes below are sourced from fetched primary sources (interviews,
transcripts, articles). Each is attributed to its specific source.

---

### On planning and career

> *"I've never had a plan."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"I'm happiest when what I'm working on is something that I couldn't have
> anticipated a few years earlier."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"This whole notion of being able to write down a five-year plan doesn't
> really hold water. Unrestricted funding lets you, at every stage, take the
> most exciting, most open-ended, highest potential value route and you can't
> just anticipate that in advance because you don't have the information."*
> — Packard Foundation interview, 2025

> *"Do what you're most excited about at any given time and not worry about
> the future and don't plan too far ahead because the world changes."*
> — Existential Hope podcast

> *"Follow your dreams. Don't worry about exactly what's going to happen in
> your future, just do what you're most excited about at any point, generally
> things will work out."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"I like to try and see 3 months out, and that gives me some intuition about
> what problems are good and what directions will be profitable."*
> — C&EN profile, 2019

### On hard problems and failure

> *"I see the most danger on working on problems that are too easy because
> everything takes work. If it's something which is too straightforward, then
> it won't really move the needle, there are probably lots and lots of other
> people doing it, so you don't really have to do that, you could do something
> else."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"A period of failure means that you're working on a problem which is really
> important, so that when you start making progress and solve it, it will be
> all the more exciting and impactful."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"Failing on your own can be kind of depressing, but if you're doing it with
> other people and you're working together and you're still not cracking the
> problem, it's like you have this feeling of the teamwork and you can solve
> the problem together."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"Don't be afraid to try to solve really hard problems because that's really
> what is important for scientists."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

### On the adjacent possible

> *"You have to choose unsolved problems which are kind of in the 'adjacent
> possible,' where they are solvable within a few years of a really talented
> graduate student working on them."*
> — Existential Hope podcast

### On open science and community

> *"If you share things, they grow and expand. If you don't share things, they
> kind of shrivel up and die."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"Scientific breakthroughs do not happen in isolation; progress depends on
> the collective contributions of many."*
> — Nobel Prize banquet speech, December 2024

> *"In science, you have so much more impact if you share what you do, invite
> other people in and encourage people who are experts in the area to join or
> stay in."*
> — Nobel Prize podcast, 2024

---

### On the communal brain and lab culture

> *"Every problem that we work on, you have to make some creative leap to
> actually solve it. So the question is, How do you build an environment where
> people will do that?"*
> — C&EN profile, 2019

> *"I don't believe in any kind of hierarchy. Anyone can have an idea that
> everyone else can listen to."*
> — C&EN profile, 2019

> *"I'm kind of fascinated by the sociology of scientific creativity."*
> — C&EN profile, 2019

> *"I think a principal investigator is a cheerleader."*
> — Reported by former student Lee Sang-min, DongA Science, 2024

> *"I believe that people do their best work and are at their most creative
> when they're happy and when they're working, really having fun working with
> their colleagues."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"If you imagine each researcher as a kind of a neuron in the communal brain,
> then people call me a connection machine."*
> — Nobel Prize podcast, 2024

### On protein design and the design space

> *"The space of possible proteins is far, far larger, astronomically larger
> than the very small set of proteins that have been explored in evolution."*
> — Existential Hope podcast

> *"In nature, proteins solve pretty much all the problems that arose during
> evolution in marvellous ways. Everything that was a challenge during
> evolution, there's a protein that kind of solves the problem."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"Our audacious idea is to bring biology out of the Stone Age through
> technological revolution in protein design."*
> — TED 2019

---

### On AI in science

> *"AI has really transformed our research. This is again an example of the
> unexpected and not planning too far ahead."*
> — Nobel Prize interview transcript, December 2024

> *"The rules [of protein folding] are still opaque, but it was pattern
> recognition that solved the problem."*
> — Nobel Prize podcast, 2024

> *"I think the extrapolation from what's been done with protein structure
> prediction and protein design, people may be over-extrapolating."*
> — Existential Hope podcast

> *"I view AI as just the latest in millennia worth of advances that humanity
> has made."*
> — Nobel Prize podcast, 2024
