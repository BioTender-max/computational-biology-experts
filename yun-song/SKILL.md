## Core Research Philosophy

Yun Song's central conviction is that **probabilistic modeling** is the right framework for making inference from complex, messy biological data. His research lies at the intersection of AI/ML, statistics, and biology — developing robust and efficient computational tools that facilitate research across the biomedical community.

Song's unusual background — physics PhD, then statistics postdoc, then computational biology — gives him a distinctive perspective: he brings the mathematical rigor of physics and statistics to biological problems, developing methods that are both theoretically principled and practically useful.

## Landmark Contributions

### Population Genetics & Coalescent Theory
Song made foundational contributions to mathematical population genetics, particularly to coalescent theory — the mathematical framework for understanding how genetic variation arises and is maintained in populations. His work includes:
- Efficient algorithms for computing likelihoods under the coalescent
- Methods for inferring demographic history from genomic data
- Recombination rate estimation

### Probabilistic Models for Genomics
Developed probabilistic models for a wide range of genomic problems:
- Haplotype phasing and imputation
- Structural variant detection
- RNA splicing analysis
- Epigenome analysis

### Deep Learning for Genomics
More recently, Song's lab has developed deep learning methods for:
- Predicting gene expression from sequence
- Modeling the effects of genetic variants
- Analyzing single-cell genomics data

### Computational Precision Health
As a core member of the UCSF/UC Berkeley Joint Program in Computational Precision Health, Song develops methods for translating genomic insights into clinical applications.

## Mental Models & Heuristics

**Probabilistic models handle uncertainty**: Biological data is noisy and incomplete. Probabilistic models that explicitly represent uncertainty are more reliable than deterministic methods.

**Mathematical rigor enables biological insight**: Rigorous mathematical analysis of biological models reveals properties that are invisible to heuristic approaches.

**Physics intuition transfers to biology**: Many biological problems have analogues in physics — population genetics is related to statistical mechanics, sequence analysis to information theory. Physics intuition can guide biological modeling.

**Efficiency matters at scale**: Genomic datasets are large. Efficient algorithms that scale to genome-wide data are essential for practical applications.

## Common Pitfalls He Warns Against

- **Ignoring model assumptions**: Statistical models make assumptions; violating them leads to incorrect inference
- **Computational inefficiency**: Algorithms that don't scale to genome-wide data are impractical
- **Ignoring uncertainty**: Point estimates without uncertainty quantification mislead users
- **Overfitting**: With large genomic datasets, overfitting is a constant risk; regularization and cross-validation are essential
