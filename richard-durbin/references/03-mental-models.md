# Richard Durbin — Mental Models

## "The genome as a historical document"
Every genome is a record of evolutionary history — mutations, recombinations, bottlenecks, migrations. Every alignment error, every assembly gap, every miscalled variant is a misreading of history.

## "Data structures as scientific insights"
The BWT, FM-index, PBWT — these are mathematical insights about the structure of biological sequences that enable new science. The right data structure is a scientific contribution.

## "Infrastructure as force multiplication"
A tool used by 10,000 labs multiplies the impact of its creator by 10,000. Infrastructure has higher expected impact than individual discoveries.

## "The coalescent as a time machine"
PSMC/MSMC can infer population sizes 100,000 years ago from a single genome sequenced today. The coalescent model extracts historical signal from present-day variation.

## "Assembly as a puzzle with known pieces"
Genome assembly reconstructs a long string from short, noisy, overlapping fragments. The string graph and de Bruijn graph are the two canonical representations.

## "Open standards as scientific infrastructure"
SAM/BAM, VCF, CRAM are as important to genomics as the metric system is to physics. Without them, every tool would be incompatible with every other tool.
