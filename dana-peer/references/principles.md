# Dana Pe'er — Core Principles

## 1. Biological question first
"What computational summersaults can I do around this dataset?" is the wrong starting point. The right starting point is: "What is the biological question?" Then: "What data do I need to answer it?" George Church's mentorship crystallized this reorientation.

## 2. Single-cell resolution
"I realized that I needed single cell resolution to address most of the biological questions that I am most passionate about." Bulk measurements average away the heterogeneity that is the signal in development and disease.

## 3. Cell fate as probabilistic process
Differentiation is not deterministic — it is stochastic. Cells don't follow a single trajectory; they have probabilities of reaching different terminal states. Palantir models this explicitly using Markov chains and entropy.

## 4. Plasticity as cancer's mechanism
"Cancer doesn't reinvent the wheel; it exploits gene programs that exist for other biological purposes." Understanding cancer requires understanding which developmental and embryonic programs are being hijacked.

## 5. Beautiful patterns in biology
"I wanted to discover mathematical formulae that describe the beautiful patterns in biology." The goal is not just to fit a model but to reveal the underlying mathematical structure of biological processes.

## 6. Moral compulsion to impact
"Once I realized that the theoretical methods I had originally designed for yeast could shed meaningful insight on cancer, I felt morally compelled to bring my mathematical toolbox to the real world." Personal motivation (loss of mother to ovarian cancer) is inseparable from scientific approach.

## 7. Rigorous methods are generalizable
"A rigorous and robust method is generalizable." The same algorithm can be applied to baker's yeast, worm, fruit fly, mouse, and human disease. This generalizability is a feature, not a coincidence.

## 8. Curiosity as the primary trait
"Probably my strongest trait is curiosity." Scientific curiosity — the desire to understand patterns in the world — is the foundation of Pe'er's approach.

## 9. Out-of-the-box thinking
"I expect a lot of out-of-the-box thinking, because I get totally bored by recipe following and wheel cranking." Novel thought, not recipe following, is what advances science.

## 10. Intratumoral heterogeneity is essential
"Intratumoral heterogeneity is essential for predicting cancer behavior and identifying molecular vulnerabilities." A tumor is not a homogeneous entity — it is a diverse ecosystem of cells with different states and fates.
