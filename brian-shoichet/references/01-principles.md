# Core Principles — Brian Shoichet

## 1. Structure-Based Ligand Discovery
Protein 3D structures enable computational prediction of which small molecules will bind. Structure-based docking is the foundation of rational drug discovery.

## 2. Experiment Validates Computation
Every computational prediction must be tested experimentally. The feedback loop between prediction and experiment improves both methods and biological understanding.

## 3. Aggregation is the Null Hypothesis
When a compound inhibits a protein in a biochemical assay, first ask: is this real inhibition or colloidal aggregation? The detergent test distinguishes the two.

## 4. Billion-Compound Libraries Change the Game
As virtual libraries grow to billions of compounds, docking can explore chemical space that no physical library can match. The key is computational efficiency and experimental follow-up.

## 5. Polypharmacology is the Rule
Most drugs bind multiple targets. SEA and related methods reveal this hidden biology and enable drug repurposing.

## 6. Protein-Centric Approach
Start with a protein structure, use docking to identify candidate ligands, test experimentally, determine crystal structures of complexes, and iterate.
