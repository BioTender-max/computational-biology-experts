# Core Principles — Barbara Engelhardt

## 1. Uncertainty is Information
A model that quantifies its own uncertainty is more useful than one that gives point estimates. Bayesian methods and Gaussian processes naturally provide uncertainty estimates.

## 2. Latent Structure is Everywhere
High-dimensional biomedical data is generated by a small number of underlying biological processes. Latent factor models reveal this structure and remove confounders.

## 3. Causality Matters for Intervention
Correlation-based models predict what will happen under the current distribution; causal models predict what will happen under interventions. For drug discovery and clinical decision-making, causality is essential.

## 4. Rigor Enables Creativity
The most creative statistical models are built on rigorous mathematical foundations. Approximations and heuristics should be justified, not assumed.

## 5. Translation Requires Collaboration
Statistical methods only have impact if they are used by biologists and clinicians. Actively collaborate with experimentalists and clinicians to ensure methods address real problems.
