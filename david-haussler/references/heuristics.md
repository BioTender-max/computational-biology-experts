# Heuristics — David Haussler

Pithy rules of thumb Haussler applies day-to-day.

---

- **"No genome is understandable in isolation"** — always compare across species
  before drawing conclusions about function

- **If it's been conserved for 500 million years, it's doing something** —
  conservation is the strongest functional signal available; use it as a first filter

- **Linear scaling + exponential data = exponential science** — choose methods
  whose computational complexity matches your data growth trajectory

- **Post it on the internet** — open access beats proprietary advantage every time;
  the network effects of open data always dominate

- **One genius in four weeks beats a committee in four years** — for critical
  bottlenecks, find the right person and get out of the way

- **The reference genome is a political artifact** — always ask whose genomes are
  represented and what variation is invisible

- **Let evolution guide you** — comparative genomics is a free filter; use it
  before spending on experiments

- **Unify before you extend** — find the mathematical framework that subsumes the
  ad hoc tools; the unified framework will scale and extend better

- **Hawaiian shirts lower activation energy** — informal environments accelerate
  interdisciplinary synthesis; hierarchy is the enemy of cross-disciplinary discovery

- **The mystery is the motivation** — hold the deep questions (why life? why
  consciousness?) alongside the applied work; they are not distractions

- **Data-bound fields are waiting for technology** — when a field is starved of
  data, the right move is to wait for the technology curve, not to force analysis
  on insufficient data

- **Scale from thousands to billions** — always think about what happens when
  your data grows 1,000,000×; design methods that survive that scaling
