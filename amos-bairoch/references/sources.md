# Sources — Amos Bairoch

## Primary Papers

### Swiss-Prot / UniProtKB

1. **Bairoch A, Boeckmann B.** "The SWISS-PROT protein sequence data bank." *Nucleic Acids Res* 19(suppl):2247–2249, 1991. DOI: [10.1093/nar/19.suppl.2247](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/19.suppl.2247)
   - The founding paper of Swiss-Prot, describing the database established in 1986.

2. **Bairoch A, Boeckmann B.** "The SWISS-PROT protein sequence data bank, recent developments." *Nucleic Acids Res* 21(13):3093–3096, 1993. DOI: [10.1093/nar/21.13.3093](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/21.13.3093)
   - Describes the three criteria distinguishing Swiss-Prot from other protein sequence databases.

3. **Bairoch A, Apweiler R.** "The SWISS-PROT protein sequence data bank and its supplement TrEMBL." *Nucleic Acids Res* 25(1):31–36, 1997. DOI: [10.1093/nar/25.1.31](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/25.1.31)
   - Introduces TrEMBL as a computer-annotated supplement to Swiss-Prot.

4. **Bairoch A, Apweiler R.** "The SWISS-PROT protein sequence database and its supplement TrEMBL in 2000." *Nucleic Acids Res* 28(1):45–48, 2000. DOI: [10.1093/nar/28.1.45](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/28.1.45)
   - Describes the Human Proteomics Initiative (HPI) to annotate all known human sequences.

### PROSITE

5. **Bairoch A.** "PROSITE: a dictionary of sites and patterns in proteins." *Nucleic Acids Res* 20(suppl):2013–2018, 1992. DOI: [10.1093/nar/20.suppl.2013](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/20.suppl.2013)
   - The founding paper of PROSITE, describing the pattern database begun in 1988.

6. **Bairoch A, Bucher P, Hofmann K.** "The PROSITE database, its status in 1997." *Nucleic Acids Res* 25(1):217–221, 1997. DOI: [10.1093/nar/25.1.217](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/25.1.217)
   - Describes the extension of PROSITE to include weight matrix profiles.

### ENZYME Database

7. **Bairoch A.** "The ENZYME data bank." *Nucleic Acids Res* 21(13):3155–3156, 1993. DOI: [10.1093/nar/21.13.3155](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/21.13.3155)
   - Founding paper of the ENZYME database, based on IUBMB nomenclature recommendations.

8. **Bairoch A.** "The ENZYME data bank in 1999." *Nucleic Acids Res* 27(1):310–311, 1999. DOI: [10.1093/nar/27.1.310](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/27.1.310)
   - Update describing ENZYME as an indispensable resource for metabolic databases.

### ExPASy

9. **Gasteiger E, Gattiker A, Hoogland C, et al.** "ExPASy: the proteomics server for in-depth protein knowledge and analysis." *Nucleic Acids Res* 31(13):3784–3788, 2003. DOI: [10.1093/nar/gkg563](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkg563)
   - Describes ExPASy as the first WWW server in the life sciences (launched 1 August 1993).

### neXtProt

10. **Lane L, Argoud-Puy G, Britan A, et al.** "neXtProt: a knowledge platform for human proteins." *Nucleic Acids Res* 40(D1):D76–D83, 2012. DOI: [10.1093/nar/gkr1179](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkr1179)
    - Founding paper of neXtProt, the human protein-centric knowledge resource.

### Cellosaurus

11. **Bairoch A.** "The Cellosaurus, a Cell-Line Knowledge Resource." *J Biomol Tech* 29(2):25–38, 2018. DOI: [10.7171/jbt.18-2902-002](https://doi.org/10.7171/jbt.18-2902-002)
    - Describes Cellosaurus, covering >100,000 cell lines used in biomedical research.

### Protein Annotation

12. **Doerks T, Bairoch A, Bork P.** "Protein annotation: detective work for function prediction." *Trends Genet* 14(6):248–250, 1998. DOI: [10.1016/s0168-9525(98)01486-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0168-9525(98)01486-3)
    - Demonstrates that automatic predictions by software robots are often erroneous; argues for careful functional annotation.

## Personal Recollections and Talks

13. **Bairoch A.** "Serendipity in bioinformatics, the tribulations of a Swiss bioinformatician through exciting times!" *Bioinformatics* 16(1):48–64, 2000. DOI: [10.1093/bioinformatics/16.1.48](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/16.1.48)
    - Personal recollection of the creation of PC/Gene, Swiss-Prot, PROSITE, and ExPASy. Primary source for Bairoch's philosophy and career narrative.

14. **Bairoch A.** "The future of annotation/biocuration." *Nature Precedings*, 2009. DOI: [10.1038/npre.2009.3092.1](https://doi.org/10.1038/npre.2009.3092.1)
    - Talk presenting Bairoch's views on manual vs. automated annotation, biocurator identity, funding, and community incentives. Primary source for many key quotes.

15. **Bairoch A.** "Bioinformatics for Human Proteomics: Current State and Future Status." *Nature Precedings*, 2010. DOI: [10.1038/npre.2010.5050.1](https://doi.org/10.1038/npre.2010.5050.1)
    - Describes the completion of Swiss-Prot human protein annotation and the motivation for neXtProt.

## Biographical and Award Sources

16. **ISCB.** "The 2025 ISCB Accomplishments by a Senior Scientist Award — Dr Amos Bairoch." *Bioinformatics* 41(Suppl 1):i3–i5, 2025. PMC: [PMC12261483](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12261483/)
    - Comprehensive biographical profile covering career trajectory, mentors, unexpected directions, and legacy.
