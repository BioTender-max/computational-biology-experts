# Michael Schatz — Mental Models

## "Long reads as a new lens"
Short reads provide a pixelated view of the genome — they can see SNPs and small indels, but structural variants are invisible. Long reads provide a higher-resolution view — they can span structural variants and reveal them directly.

## "Structural variants as the dark matter of the genome"
Most genomics studies focus on SNPs and small indels, but structural variants — which affect much larger regions of the genome — are often more functionally important. They are there, they matter, but they are hard to see with standard tools.

## "The genome as a structure, not just a sequence"
The genome is not just a linear sequence — it is a three-dimensional structure with chromatin organization, topologically associating domains, and long-range regulatory interactions.

## "Cloud computing as a force multiplier"
Cloud computing enables genomics analysis at scales impossible on local clusters. A researcher with access to cloud computing can analyze 10,000 genomes in the time it would take to analyze 100 on a local cluster.

## "Plant genomics as an underexplored frontier"
Plant genomes are often large, polyploid, and repeat-rich — making them challenging to assemble. But they are also economically important (food security) and scientifically interesting (evolution, adaptation).

## "Diversity as a scientific necessity"
Most human genomics studies have been conducted in populations of European ancestry. This limits the generalizability of findings and misses genetic variants that are common in other populations.
