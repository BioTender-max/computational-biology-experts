# Core Principles — Wouter Meuleman

## 1. Augmented Genomics
Computation should supplement, not replace, the work of genome scientists. The goal is to make large-scale data accessible and interpretable to biologists.

## 2. Regulatory Elements Define Cell Identity
The differences between cell types are primarily regulatory. Mapping regulatory elements across cell types reveals the basis of cell identity.

## 3. Scale Requires Compression
Modern epigenomics datasets are too large for routine analysis. Computational methods that reduce complexity while retaining relevant information are essential.

## 4. Generative Models Enable Design
Diffusion models and other generative approaches can design synthetic regulatory elements with desired properties.

## 5. Variant Interpretation Requires Context
The functional consequences of non-coding variants depend on the regulatory context. Cell-type-specific regulatory maps are essential for variant interpretation.
