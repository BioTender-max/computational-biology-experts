# Bonnie Berger — Core Principles (Extended)

## P1: Follow the Interesting Problems
"My question is: what are the problems in biology that math can be applied to?" Not: what math do I know? This inverts the usual academic specialization logic. Berger entered protein folding because her advisor was excited about it; she entered comparative genomics because the data was there; she entered privacy because the need was urgent. Follow the problem, not the tool.

## P2: Exploit Biological Redundancy
Biological data is not random. Genomic sequences cluster in dense groups (evolutionary tree is not too bushy). Gene expression has modular structure. Protein sequences have fold families. These structures can be exploited algorithmically — compressive genomics, compressed sensing for transcriptomics, network alignment.

## P3: Cross-Disciplinary Mash-Up as Method
Cryptography → protein structure prediction (pairwise residue correlations). PageRank → comparative genomics (IsoRank). Language models → viral escape (Mad Libs for viruses). Topology → virus assembly. The best biological insights come from unexpected mathematical analogies. "Everything informs everything else to make it better."

## P4: Data, Data, Data
The theme of Berger's work. Biological data is the raw material; mathematics is the tool. As data grows, algorithms must scale. Compressive genomics was invented because data was growing faster than compute.

## P5: Build for Scale
Algorithms must keep pace with data generation. Compressive acceleration: runtime proportional to unique data, not total data. The relative advantage of compressive algorithms grows as databases grow. Design for the data volumes of the future.

## P6: Privacy as Scientific Constraint
Genomic data sharing requires cryptographic protection. Multi-party computation allows institutions to jointly train models on encrypted data. Privacy is not just a legal constraint — it is a scientific one that determines what data can be used.

## P7: Network View Integrates Heterogeneous Data
"Our idea was that taking a network view allows us to analyze different kinds of data through a common lens." IsoRank and Mashup integrate sequence, interaction, expression, and pharmacogenomic data through network topology.

## P8: Curiosity-Driven Research
"I only work on what gets me excited." Berger's research agenda is driven by curiosity, not by funding trends or field prestige. This produces a portfolio that is genuinely diverse and genuinely deep.

## P9: Mentorship as Scientific Multiplier
Students lead in new directions. Match them to problems they are excited about. A lab's strength comes from diversity of expertise and mutual support. "I listen to them. I see what they're good at, who they are."

## P10: Hyperfocus as Productivity
"Allot four hours a day where you're going to work with no interruptions." Sustained, uninterrupted attention is the scarce resource in modern academic life. Protect it.
