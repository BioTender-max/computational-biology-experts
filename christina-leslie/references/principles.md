# Christina Leslie — Core Principles

Ranked by cross-source frequency across interviews, lab pages, and papers.

## 1. Ask the right question first
"The most important thing is asking the right question in the first place." Mathematicians are attached to elegant solutions; biologists need meaningful insights. The transition from mathematics to computational biology requires reorienting from "what can I solve?" to "what should I ask?"

## 2. Global, data-driven perspective
"Study cellular biological systems from a global and data-driven perspective." Don't study one gene at a time. Use genome-wide, data-driven approaches to understand molecular networks underlying fundamental cellular processes.

## 3. Discriminative over generative models
Predictive models trained to discriminate between positive and negative examples are more powerful than generative models for regulatory genomics. The goal is prediction, not description. String kernels: discriminate between functional and non-functional sequences.

## 4. Integrate multiple data modalities
Gene regulation involves sequence, chromatin accessibility, 3D genome structure, and transcription factor binding. Integrate all available data types. GraphReg: 3D genome-aware gene expression prediction.

## 5. Machine learning learns from data
"Algorithms that 'learn' from data to build a model that can be used to make accurate predictions." Machine learning is not magic — it is a principled approach to learning patterns from labeled examples.

## 6. Collaborate with experimental labs
Close collaboration with wet-lab scientists at MSKCC is essential for both validation and problem formulation. Computational predictions must be validated experimentally (CRISPRi, TAP-seq).

## 7. Ambition in cancer biology
"Scientists are encouraged to be ambitious and tackle big questions." MSKCC's culture encourages tackling the most important problems in cancer, not just the most tractable ones.

## 8. Elegant algorithms in service of biology
Mathematicians like elegant solutions; biologists need meaningful insights. Mathematical elegance is secondary to biological insight.

## 9. Post-transcriptional regulation matters
microRNA-mediated gene silencing; competition between miRNAs and target transcripts (ceRNA hypothesis). First systems-level analyses of microRNA competition.

## 10. 3D genome organization shapes regulation
GraphReg: chromatin interactions for gene expression prediction. Distal enhancers regulate gene expression through 3D chromatin interactions; models that ignore 3D structure miss key regulatory logic.
