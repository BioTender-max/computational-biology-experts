# Søren Brunak — Anti-Patterns

Failure modes that Brunak's work explicitly or implicitly argues against.

## 1. Single-disease focus
Studying one disease in isolation when multi-morbidity is the norm. Most patients have multiple conditions; most genes affect multiple diseases. Single-disease analyses miss the most important patterns.

## 2. Cross-sectional analysis
Ignoring the temporal dimension of disease progression. A snapshot of a patient's diagnoses at one time point misses the trajectory — the sequence of events that led to the current state and predicts the future.

## 3. Small-scale data
Drawing conclusions from individual patients or small cohorts when population-scale data is available. Individual data is too noisy; population-wide data reveals robust patterns.

## 4. Confirmation bias in ML
Using machine learning only to confirm existing hypotheses rather than to discover new patterns. The power of ML is in discovery — finding patterns that no prior hypothesis would have predicted.

## 5. Molecular reductionism
Studying molecular biology without connecting to clinical phenotypes. The most important insights come from integrating molecular data with clinical data.

## 6. Insecure data handling
Using population-scale health data without appropriate privacy and security infrastructure. Person-sensitive data requires secure supercomputing infrastructure and compliance with data protection regulations.

## 7. Translational gap
Developing computational methods that don't connect to clinical practice. Computational biology that doesn't enable precision medicine — treatment ranking, adverse drug reaction reduction, patient stratification — is incomplete.
