# Søren Brunak — Core Principles

Ranked by cross-source frequency across interviews, lab pages, and papers.

## 1. Integrate molecular and clinical data
Disease Systems Biology: connect molecular-level systems biology (proteins, genes, pathways) with phenotypic data from the healthcare sector (EHRs, diagnoses, drug prescriptions). Neither alone is sufficient for understanding disease.

## 2. Longitudinal perspective on disease
Disease is not a static state — it is a trajectory through time. Analyze disease progression patterns over years and decades, not just cross-sectional snapshots. Events separated by long time periods must be taken into account.

## 3. Population-scale analysis
Individual patient data is noisy; population-wide data (millions of patients, decades of records) reveals robust patterns. Denmark's unique registry infrastructure — 6.2–7.2 million patients, 14.9 years of data — enables this.

## 4. Machine learning as discovery tool
Pioneer of ML in biology since the mid-1980s. Machine learning can extract signal from limited data if the features are right (SignalP). As data grows, the methods scale. Use ML in discovery mode, not just confirmation mode.

## 5. Multi-disciplinary combination
The impact of Brunak's research comes from combining computer technology, physics, biology, biomedical insights, and clinical knowledge. CBS at DTU was built on this principle: molecular biologists, biochemists, MDs, physicists, and computer scientists working together.

## 6. Translational utility
Methods must be useful in precision medicine — ranking treatment options, reducing adverse drug reactions, stratifying patients. Computational biology that doesn't connect to clinical practice is incomplete.

## 7. Multi-morbidity as the norm
Genes and pathways play roles in multiple diseases. Multi-morbidity is not a coincidence — it reflects the pleiotropic nature of genes and proteins. Design analyses that capture it.

## 8. Secure infrastructure for sensitive data
Population-wide health data is extraordinarily valuable but extraordinarily sensitive. Secure supercomputing infrastructure is required to handle person-sensitive data at scale.

## 9. Prevention over treatment
The ultimate goal of disease trajectory analysis is not just to describe disease progression but to intervene before it happens. Prediction enables prevention. Vision: a healthcare system focused on prevention.

## 10. Gene pleiotropy and protein multi-functionality
Many genes rationalize clinically observed multi-morbidity patterns. Numerous examples of gene pleiotropy and protein multi-functionality presumably await discovery.
