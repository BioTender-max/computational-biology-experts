# Søren Brunak — Mental Models

## 1. "Complement classical epidemiology towards disease-spectrum wide analyses in a lifelong perspective"
Classical epidemiology studies one disease at a time, in cross-sectional snapshots. Brunak's approach: analyze the entire disease spectrum, longitudinally, across a lifetime. This reveals patterns invisible to traditional methods — diseases that are causally linked but separated by years or decades.

## 2. "Genes and pathways play roles in more than one disease"
Multi-morbidity is not a coincidence — it reflects the pleiotropic nature of genes and proteins. Understanding why certain diseases co-occur requires understanding the shared molecular mechanisms. This is the molecular basis of disease trajectory analysis.

## 3. "Condensing millions of individual trajectories into a smaller set of recurrent ones"
The goal is not to describe every patient's unique trajectory but to identify the common patterns that recur across the population. These recurrent trajectories are the signal; individual variation is the noise. Statistical significance filters out the noise.

## 4. "A vision of a future healthcare system focused on prevention"
The ultimate goal of disease trajectory analysis is not just to describe disease progression but to intervene before it happens. Prediction enables prevention. The healthcare system of the future will be proactive, not reactive.

## 5. "Combining scientific disciplines in novel ways"
The impact of Brunak's research comes from combining computer technology, physics, biology, biomedical insights, and clinical knowledge. No single discipline could have produced these results. The CBS at DTU was built on this principle.

## 6. "Population-wide registries as a scientific resource"
Denmark's unique registry infrastructure — covering the entire population for decades — is a scientific resource of extraordinary value. Using it responsibly, with appropriate privacy and security infrastructure, enables discoveries impossible elsewhere.
