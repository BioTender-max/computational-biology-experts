# Bernhard Schölkopf — Core Principles

Ranked by cross-source frequency across interviews, papers, and books.

## 1. Causation over correlation
"Current models only pay attention to correlations, ignoring causality." Statistical dependences are not causal relationships. A model that learns correlations will fail under distribution shift. Causal models explicitly model interventions.

## 2. Find structure in the world
"What really interests me is how we — or how any intelligent systems, humans or machines — can find structure in the world. Why does the world look structured and regular rather than random? How do we find these regularities?" This is the fundamental question driving all of Schölkopf's work.

## 3. Model interventions explicitly
Causal models answer "what would happen if we intervened?" Statistical models only answer "what is correlated with what?" For biology and medicine, interventions are what matter.

## 4. Independent Causal Mechanisms (ICM) principle
The causal mechanisms that generate data are independent of each other. This independence can be exploited for causal discovery and transfer learning.

## 5. Kernel methods for non-linearity
Map data into a high-dimensional feature space (reproducing kernel Hilbert space) where linear methods apply. The kernel trick makes this computationally tractable.

## 6. Academic independence
"There's a reason why in the end many of the most surprising developments come from academia — I think it may be related to tenure, which gives people complete freedom, stability and independence."

## 7. Broad intellectual curiosity
Physics, mathematics, philosophy, archaeology, anthropology all inform each other. Machine learning is a universal tool — apply it across all domains.

## 8. Transfer learning requires causality
Humans transfer knowledge across domains; machines don't — because they lack causal models. Causal representations are stable across interventions and distribution shifts.

## 9. Causal representation learning
The key open problem in AI: learning high-level causal variables from low-level observations. This is the bridge between statistical learning and genuine understanding.

## 10. European AI ecosystem
ELLIS: keeping Europe competitive in AI. Academic institutions need to be able to compete with industry for talent and resources.
