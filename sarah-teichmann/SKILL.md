## Foundational Philosophy

### The Human Cell Atlas as a Reference Map
Just as the Human Genome Project created a reference sequence for human DNA, the Human Cell Atlas is creating a reference map of all human cell types. This map will transform our understanding of human biology and disease by providing a baseline against which to compare diseased states, developmental abnormalities, and drug effects. The HCA is not a single experiment — it is a global consortium of thousands of scientists contributing data from every human tissue.

### Cellular Diversity is Generated by Combinatorial Gene Regulation
The ~37 trillion cells in the human body are all derived from a single fertilized egg, yet they are extraordinarily diverse. This diversity is generated by combinatorial gene regulation: different combinations of transcription factors activate different gene expression programs. Single-cell genomics reveals this combinatorial logic by measuring gene expression in individual cells, enabling the identification of novel cell states that are invisible to bulk measurements.

### The Immune System as a Model for Cellular Diversity
The immune system is the most complex tissue in the human body, with hundreds of distinct cell types that communicate through an elaborate network of cytokines, receptors, and cell-cell contacts. Understanding immune cell diversity — how it is generated, maintained, and dysregulated in disease — is the central challenge of immunology. Single-cell genomics has transformed this field by enabling the systematic characterization of immune cell states across tissues, developmental stages, and disease conditions.

### Spatial Context is Essential for Understanding Tissue Function
Cells do not function in isolation — they function in the context of their tissue environment. The spatial organization of cells within a tissue determines which cells communicate with each other, which signals they receive, and how they respond. Spatial transcriptomics methods (Visium, MERFISH, Xenium) capture this spatial context, enabling the study of tissue architecture at single-cell resolution.

## Core Technical Frameworks

### Human Cell Atlas Data Generation
**Tissue dissociation:** Enzymatic and mechanical dissociation of fresh or frozen tissue into single-cell suspensions. Critical step: minimize stress-induced gene expression changes during dissociation.

**Single-cell RNA-seq:** 10x Genomics Chromium (most common), Smart-seq2 (full-length, lower throughput), Parse Biosciences (combinatorial indexing). Choice depends on throughput, sensitivity, and cost requirements.

**Single-nucleus RNA-seq:** For frozen tissues or tissues that are difficult to dissociate (brain, heart, muscle). Nuclei are isolated instead of whole cells. Lower RNA content but enables analysis of previously inaccessible tissues.

**Spatial transcriptomics:** Visium (10x Genomics) for whole-transcriptome spatial profiling; MERFISH/Xenium for targeted, high-resolution spatial profiling. Enables mapping of cell types to tissue locations.

### Protein Complex Assembly Principles (Early Work)
Teichmann's early work established that protein complexes assemble via stereotypical, conserved pathways:
- **Heterodimers** form first, then assemble into larger complexes
- **Symmetric complexes** (homodimers, homotetramers) are more common than asymmetric ones
- **Assembly pathways** are conserved across species
- **"Periodic table of protein complexes"** — a systematic classification of all possible protein complex topologies

This structural biology background informs Teichmann's approach to single-cell data: just as protein complexes have conserved assembly pathways, cell types have conserved developmental trajectories.

### Immune Cell Atlas Methods
**T cell receptor (TCR) sequencing:** 10x Genomics VDJ sequencing captures TCR sequences alongside gene expression. Enables clonotype analysis: which T cells are clonally expanded? Which clonotypes are shared across tissues?

**B cell receptor (BCR) sequencing:** Analogous to TCR sequencing for B cells. Enables analysis of antibody repertoires and somatic hypermutation.

**Cell-cell communication analysis:** CellChat, NicheNet, LIANA — tools for inferring ligand-receptor interactions between cell types from single-cell data. Enables reconstruction of the communication network within a tissue.

**Cross-tissue immune analysis:** Comparing immune cell states across tissues (blood, lymph node, spleen, gut, lung) reveals tissue-resident vs. circulating immune cells and tissue-specific immune adaptations.

### Spatial Transcriptomics Analysis
**Cell type deconvolution:** Each Visium spot contains multiple cells. Deconvolution methods (cell2location, RCTD, Stereoscope) estimate the proportion of each cell type in each spot.

**Spatially variable genes:** Genes whose expression varies systematically with spatial position. Moran's I statistic measures spatial autocorrelation.

**Spatial domains:** Tissue regions with distinct gene expression profiles. Identified by clustering spots based on gene expression + spatial coordinates.

**Cell-cell communication in space:** Ligand-receptor interactions between spatially adjacent cells. More biologically meaningful than bulk ligand-receptor analysis.

## Landmark Contributions

### Protein Complex Assembly Pathways (Nature, 2006)
Levy, Boeri Erba, Robinson, Teichmann — "Assembly reflects evolution of protein complexes." Showed that protein complexes assemble via conserved, stereotypical pathways. Established the structural biology foundation of Teichmann's research program.

### Periodic Table of Protein Complexes (Science, 2015)
Ahnert, Marsh, Hernández, Robinson, Teichmann — "Principles of assembly reveal a periodic table of protein complexes." Systematic classification of all possible protein complex topologies. Showed that only a small fraction of possible topologies are observed in nature.

### Human Cell Atlas Founding (2016)
Regev, Teichmann, et al. — "The Human Cell Atlas." Science 358:eaam9868. Articulated the vision and plan for the HCA. Co-founded the consortium with Aviv Regev. Now includes 3,000+ members worldwide.

### Maternal-Fetal Interface (Nature, 2018)
Vento-Tormo, Efremova, Botting, et al. — "Single-cell reconstruction of the early maternal-fetal interface in humans." First single-cell atlas of the maternal-fetal interface. Identified novel cell types and communication networks at the placenta. Revealed how the fetus evades maternal immune rejection.

### Human Thymus Atlas (Nature, 2020)
Park, Brealey, Gomes, et al. — "A cell atlas of human thymic development defines T cell repertoire formation." Comprehensive single-cell atlas of human thymus development. Identified novel thymic cell types and the developmental trajectory of T cells.

### Human Heart Cell Atlas (Nature, 2022)
Kanemaru, Cranley, et al. — "Spatially resolved multiomics of human cardiac niches." Combined single-cell and spatial transcriptomics to map the human heart at single-cell resolution.

## Heuristics & Rules of Thumb

1. **Dissociation stress is a major confounder.** Genes induced by dissociation stress (heat shock genes, immediate early genes) should be identified and regressed out. Use cold dissociation protocols when possible.

2. **Single-nucleus RNA-seq for frozen tissues.** For tissues that cannot be freshly dissociated (archived samples, brain, muscle), single-nucleus RNA-seq is the method of choice.

3. **Integrate TCR/BCR data with gene expression.** Clonotype analysis reveals which T/B cells are clonally expanded and which are responding to specific antigens. This is essential for understanding adaptive immune responses.

4. **Validate spatial deconvolution with known markers.** After deconvolution, check that cell types are in the expected tissue locations (e.g., T cells in T cell zones of lymph nodes).

5. **The HCA is a resource, not just a paper.** The Human Cell Atlas data portal (data.humancellatlas.org) provides access to processed single-cell data from hundreds of tissues. Use it as a reference before generating new data.

6. **Cell-cell communication analysis requires careful interpretation.** Ligand-receptor co-expression does not prove communication. Always validate key interactions with orthogonal methods (FACS, functional assays).

---

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

**The Dissociation Artifact:** Genes induced by dissociation stress (HSP70, FOS, JUN) will appear as differentially expressed between cell types if not corrected. Always check for dissociation stress signatures.

**The Doublet Contamination:** Droplet-based methods capture doublets (two cells in one droplet) at a rate of ~1% per 1,000 cells. Doublets appear as cells with mixed gene expression profiles. Use DoubletFinder or Scrublet to identify and remove doublets.

**The Ambient RNA Problem:** Ambient RNA from lysed cells contaminates all droplets. This can cause false positive gene expression in cell types that don't express those genes. Use SoupX or CellBender to correct for ambient RNA.

**The Tissue-Specific Bias:** Cell types that are rare in a tissue will be underrepresented in single-cell data. Enrichment strategies (FACS sorting, targeted capture) are needed to study rare cell types.

**The Cross-Tissue Comparison Pitfall:** Comparing cell types across tissues requires careful normalization and integration. The same cell type (e.g., macrophages) can have very different gene expression profiles in different tissues due to tissue-specific adaptations.
