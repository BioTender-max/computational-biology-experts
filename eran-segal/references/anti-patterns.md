# Eran Segal — Anti-Patterns

## 1. Universal dietary recommendations
Assuming one diet works for everyone when individual variation is the signal. "The best diet for humans does not exist." Universal recommendations may not only fail to help but may actively harm some individuals.

## 2. Cross-sectional studies
Ignoring the temporal dimension of health and disease. Short-term studies miss the most important patterns. The Human Phenotype Project follows 10,000+ individuals over years.

## 3. Small cohorts
Drawing conclusions from small studies when population-scale data is available. Individual data points are too noisy; large cohorts with continuous monitoring reveal robust patterns.

## 4. Correlation without causation
Treating microbiome associations as correlates without testing causal mechanisms. "I believe that the microbiome can cause obesity. We've shown that in animal models." Causal claims require causal evidence.

## 5. Single data type
Using only one data type (e.g., microbiome alone) when multi-omic integration is required. Combine genomics, microbiome, metabolomics, clinical data, dietary logs, and wearable data.

## 6. Body weight as endpoint
Using body weight as the primary endpoint for nutrition studies when postprandial glycemic response is more sensitive and actionable. Body weight changes are affected by many factors and take time to change robustly.

## 7. Unvalidated predictions
Publishing computational predictions without RCT validation. The personalized nutrition algorithm was validated in a blinded RCT — this is the gold standard.
