# Eugene Myers — Source-Verified Quotes

20 quotes with source attribution. All verified from primary sources.

## On Tools and Impact

1. "Having the best tools is really what the game is all about."
   — *MPG Portrait, "Source of Life"*

2. "I produce the tools that allow molecular biologists to discover the things that they discover."
   — *Caltech Heritage Project interview*

3. "A computer is a programmable device, and once programmed, is a specific device for doing something that I conceived of. I found this magical."
   — *ISCB Senior Scientist Award, PLOS Computational Biology, 2014*

## On Algorithm Design and BLAST

4. "I was actually trying to do theory. That's what led to BLAST, the search engine. BLAST was basically just a heuristic version of a theoretical result that I was producing, around 1990."
   — *Caltech Heritage Project interview*

5. "I didn't even realize how popular that paper was, actually. [laughs] I was on it, and it wasn't until I think — that paper came out in 1990 — it wasn't until like 1994 when there was the U.S. News Report about our department at Arizona... we were suddenly number one in citations per faculty member."
   — *Caltech Heritage Project interview*

6. "I still only feel as good as my last result, most days."
   — *Caltech Heritage Project interview*

## On Genome Assembly

7. "When we proposed to shotgun-sequence the human genome, most people thought it was a crazy idea."
   — *MPG Portrait, "Source of Life"*

8. "That was the most stressful time of my life, but also the most exciting."
   — *MPG Portrait, "Source of Life"* (on the Celera human genome project)

9. "I first started thinking about this problem in 1984... the problem that has and continues to fascinate me to this day is sequence assembly."
   — *ISCB Senior Scientist Award, PLOS Computational Biology, 2014*

10. "My favorite problem, yet fully solved in my opinion, is DNA sequence assembly — it is a combinatorially very rich string problem."
    — *ACGT 101 Questions with a Bioinformatician, 2016*

## On Data Philosophy

11. "Generate it, analyze it, throw it away. If you don't like it a week later, then do it again."
    — *"Shifting Ground for Big Data Researchers" profile*

12. "If keeping the data is more expensive [than regenerating it], repeat the experiment rather than store the data."
    — *"Shifting Ground for Big Data Researchers" profile*

13. "I think the hardest issue for biologists is psychological. I'm a mathematician, so it's not so hard for me. You have to just run the numbers. It's just a financial decision."
    — *"Shifting Ground for Big Data Researchers" profile*

## On Biology and Physics

14. "I'm really bored with networks and -omics. Stamp collecting large parts lists seems to have become the norm despite the fact that it rarely leads to much mechanistic insight. Without an understanding of spatial organization and soft-matter physics, most important biological phenomenon cannot be explained."
    — *ACGT 101 Questions with a Bioinformatician, 2016*

15. "I don't think so much that they have to be computational. I think it's more they have to be physicists. I think physics is probably more important than computation."
    — *Caltech Heritage Project interview* (on what future molecular biologists need)

16. "Understanding what a cell does is not just a question of biochemistry. It's a question of biochemistry and physics."
    — *Caltech Heritage Project interview*

## On Career and Interdisciplinary Work

17. "Learn mathematics and programming now while your mind is young and supple, you can acquire a large corpus of knowledge about biological processes later."
    — *ACGT 101 Questions with a Bioinformatician, 2016*

18. "I want to know how genetics produce the diverse forms that life takes. For example, how do genes determine how the brain of the Drosophila fruit fly is wired and functions?"
    — *MPG Portrait, "Source of Life"*

19. "A lot of people talk about working across disciplines, but people are either naïve about the cultural differences, and the fact that they have to be attuned to the cultural differences in the sciences. We're not talking in sociological terms; we're talking about the culture of different scientific fields. Because they have cultures. They really do."
    — *Caltech Heritage Project interview*

20. "I just love writing program codes!"
    — *MPG Portrait, "Source of Life"*
