## On AI as the Ultimate Tool for Science

1. "My aim was, as always, to build AI algorithms that were general enough to eventually be able to be applied to scientific problems like protein folding."
   — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

2. "I believe it could be the ultimate tool to help with science. To use AI systems to find patterns in data, insights in data and structure, and then help us advance scientific knowledge."
   — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

3. "I've been driven my whole life to build AI as the ultimate tool for sciences because I want to understand the deepest mysteries of physics, of the universe."
   — *Die Zeit interview, January 2025*

4. "The fundamental drive has always been to build AI to accelerate science, medicine, and our understanding of the world."
   — *Sequoia/HTX interview*

## On Biology and AI

5. "Biology at its most fundamental level can be thought of as an information processing system albeit a phenomenally complex and emergent one. AI is potentially the perfect description language for biology."
   — *Nobel lecture, December 2024*

6. "If you take physics, mathematics is the perfect descriptive language for it. But biology is an interactive emergent system, far too complex to describe with a few equations. AI is actually the right analysis approach for such a system — messy, interactive, lots of weak signals, and then something emergent happens out of it."
   — *Die Zeit interview, January 2025*

7. "AlphaFold is a proof point that could usher in a new era of 'digital biology'... We sometimes think of this as doing Science at Digital Speed."
   — *Nobel lecture, December 2024*

## On Interdisciplinary Research

8. "I'm a really big believer in interdisciplinary research and interdisciplinary science. The next 10 years or so, a lot of the big advances will come in the combination of two or more subject areas and sort of the in-between points where they connect."
   — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

9. "What that means is being quite a generalist and a sort of polymath in a way, and getting quite deep understanding and knowledge of at least two areas, which is quite hard to do, but I think it's extremely worthwhile."
   — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

## On Open Science

10. "We are huge believers in open science and open access... the way that science progresses quickly is the sharing of ideas, the critiquing of each other's ideas and people building on top of other people's work."
    — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

11. "We knew that we could only do a tiny, tiny fraction of the downstream work that AlphaFold could enable. We are trying to do some of that ourselves with drug discovery and so on, but it's been amazing to see what hundreds of thousands of researchers around the world have been doing with AlphaFold that we could never have imagined or never even heard of."
    — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

## On Failure and Scientific Process

12. "If you design experiments well, then either answer — whether it works or it doesn't work — is actually useful for your next hypothesis. I sort of think of it as splitting the hypothesis space into two, and you keep splitting it into two."
    — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

13. "I don't even think of it as failure. I just think of it as a necessary part of the scientific process."
    — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

## On Timing and Problem Selection

14. "You need to pick hard problems to solve and be ambitious with that, but you've also got to pick the right time when the world and the context that you are in is the right kind of environment for those ideas to flourish."
    — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

15. "I've described it as being 20 years ahead of your time."
    — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

## On Passion and Career

16. "I think this is where intrinsic passion for what you're doing becomes important... I was going to do it no matter what, because I think it's the most fascinating technology to try and develop."
    — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*

17. "I don't really think of things as work-life balance. I guess I've been very lucky in that I've designed my work to sort of be my life passions."

18. "Perhaps I had less faith in that than I should have had. That those ideas would bear fruit, with enough passion and work and effort. I think having confidence in your ideas from an early stage is very, very important."

## On AGI and the Future

19. "I identify myself as a scientist first and foremost. The whole reason I'm doing everything I've done in my life is in the pursuit of knowledge and trying to understand the world around us."
    — *TIME100, April 2025*

20. "I think AI will be more involved [in Nobel Prizes]. I think for the next decade or more, human ingenuity will still be required to come up with the theory. And then very smart people will use AI to help them solve it."
    — *Die Zeit interview, January 2025*

21. "Technology as transformative as AGI requires exceptional care and foresight."
    — *Nobel lecture, December 2024*

22. "We don't understand anything about the nature of reality really. We have some approximations, but they have holes in them. Quantum mechanics is a little bit strange in many ways. We don't know what the nature of time is. I don't understand why people aren't more troubled by this."
    — *Die Zeit interview, January 2025*

## On Games and Intelligence

23. "What makes for a suitable problem for AI? Massive combinatorial search space. Clear objective function. Either lots of data and/or an accurate and efficient simulator."
    — *Nobel lecture, December 2024*

24. "Move 37 was maybe the first example of that ever in AI. It wasn't just that we won that match. It was because AlphaGo made these creative moves. That was, for me, the most surprising and beautiful thing about it."
    — *Die Zeit interview, January 2025*

25. "The science of AI is about trying to explore and understand what intelligence is. And the best expression of understanding something is actually trying to build it."
    — *Nobel Prize interview, December 2024*
