# Demis Hassabis — Anti-Patterns

Seven failure modes that Hassabis explicitly warns against or has personally experienced.

---

## Anti-Pattern 1 — Premature Timing (Being 20 Years Ahead)

**Description:** Pursuing a technically correct idea before the enabling conditions (data, compute, methods, talent) are ready. Hassabis's first games company, Elixir Studios, failed partly because its ambitions exceeded what the technology of the time could support.

**Hassabis's diagnosis:** "I've described it as being 20 years ahead of your time. Actually, what I realised was that timing is very important."

**The trap:** Confusing the quality of an idea with the readiness of the ecosystem. A great idea at the wrong time is a failed project.

**Correction:** Map enabling conditions explicitly. If multiple conditions are not yet met, invest in building them rather than forcing the project.

---

## Anti-Pattern 2 — Narrow Benchmarks That Miss the Point

**Description:** Optimizing for benchmarks that don't reflect real-world utility. Hassabis was critical of the ARC benchmark being labeled an "AGI test" — it is a "rather mundane puzzle, nothing more."

**Hassabis's diagnosis:** "It is a pretty big misnomer... That does not have a real belonging in rigorous science. Then journalists get confused, the general public gets confused."

**The trap:** Benchmark inflation — claiming that performance on a narrow test implies general capability. This misleads the field and erodes trust.

**Correction:** Define benchmarks that reflect the actual capability you care about. For protein folding, the threshold was <1.0Å error — the level of precision that matters to experimentalists.

---

## Anti-Pattern 3 — Closed Science

**Description:** Keeping tools, data, and predictions proprietary when open release would multiply impact. Hassabis explicitly rejected this for AlphaFold.

**Hassabis's diagnosis:** "We knew that we could only do a tiny, tiny fraction of the downstream work that AlphaFold could enable."

**The trap:** Treating scientific tools as competitive moats. This maximizes short-term advantage while minimizing long-term impact.

**Correction:** Open-source core scientific tools. Retain competitive advantage in applications, not in the underlying science.

---

## Anti-Pattern 4 — AGI Hype Inflation

**Description:** Using "AGI" loosely to describe systems that exhibit narrow capabilities. This confuses the public, misleads policymakers, and makes it harder to have serious conversations about real risks.

**Hassabis's diagnosis:** "I can just write something on a piece of paper and call it AGI. Does that make it an AGI test? No."

**The trap:** Marketing language bleeding into scientific discourse. Once "AGI" loses its meaning, it becomes impossible to reason clearly about when we've actually achieved it.

**Correction:** Use precise definitions. AGI = a system that exhibits all cognitive capabilities that humans possess, able to learn for itself how to accomplish tasks. Test against tens of thousands of cognitive benchmarks, not one.

---

## Anti-Pattern 5 — Ignoring Dual-Use Risks

**Description:** Releasing powerful AI tools without consulting biosecurity, bioethics, and safety experts. Before releasing AlphaFold, DeepMind consulted 30+ experts specifically to assess dual-use risks.

**Hassabis's diagnosis:** "There are two big risks. One is bad actors repurposing these general technologies for harmful acts... Second is the technical risk inherent in AGI."

**The trap:** Assuming that because your intentions are good, the technology will only be used for good. General-purpose tools are, by definition, general-purpose.

**Correction:** Build safety review into the development process, not as a post-hoc check. The more powerful the tool, the earlier this review must begin.

---

## Anti-Pattern 6 — Passive Engagement with Societal Implications

**Description:** Treating ethics and policy engagement as someone else's problem. Hassabis believes scientists working on transformative technologies have an active obligation to engage with governments, academia, and civil society.

**Hassabis's diagnosis:** "It's important for technologists and scientists like us who are working on the technology, to engage widely with civil society, governments, academia, industry, to come together to start debating these questions."

**The trap:** Retreating to the lab and leaving policy to non-experts. This creates a vacuum that gets filled by people who don't understand the technology.

**Correction:** Proactively engage with policymakers, the public, and civil society. This is not a distraction from research — it is part of the responsibility of working on transformative technology.

---

## Anti-Pattern 7 — Celebrating Success Instead of Compounding It

**Description:** Treating each achievement as a destination rather than a launchpad. Hassabis acknowledges he is "not that good at celebrating successes" — each one immediately motivates the next, bigger ambition.

**Hassabis's diagnosis:** "Each time I've had success in my career, it's just made me more motivated for the next thing to think even bigger. So it means I'm not that good at celebrating successes. Perhaps I need to get better at that."

**The trap:** Resting on achievements. In a fast-moving field, yesterday's breakthrough is today's baseline.

**Correction:** Use success as fuel. Celebrate briefly, then immediately ask: what does this enable that wasn't possible before?
