# Demis Hassabis — Mental Models

Six core mental models that shape how Hassabis perceives problems and makes decisions.

---

## Mental Model 1 — The Energy Landscape Topology

**Source:** Nobel lecture

Proteins don't randomly search all possible conformations (Levinthal's Paradox: 10^300 conformations would take longer than the age of the universe to enumerate). Instead, the energy landscape has topology — valleys, ridges, funnels — that guides folding to the minimum energy state within milliseconds.

**The model:** Hard problems that seem intractable by brute force often have hidden structure — a topology in the solution space — that makes them tractable if you can learn that structure.

**Application:** Before declaring a problem "too hard for AI," ask: is there topology in the solution space? Can a model learn that topology from data? AlphaFold's success was predicated on the answer being yes.

**Key quote:** "Must mean there's some topology in the energy landscape that guides the folding process."

---

## Mental Model 2 — Hypothesis Space Bisection

**Source:** Nobel interview

Every well-designed experiment bisects the hypothesis space. Whether it succeeds or fails, it eliminates half the possibilities and points toward the next question.

**The model:** Scientific progress is not a linear march toward truth — it is a binary search tree. Each experiment is a node that splits the remaining uncertainty in two.

**Application:** Design experiments so that both outcomes are informative. If a negative result tells you nothing, the experiment was poorly designed. The goal is maximum information per experiment, not maximum probability of success.

**Key quote:** "I sort of think of it as splitting the hypothesis space into two, and you keep splitting it into two, and it doesn't really matter if in any particular one experiment it didn't work or it did work, it still helps you advance overall to the next question."

## Mental Model 3 — Intelligence as Civilizational Force

**Source:** Nobel interview, CBS 60 Minutes

Human intelligence, operating through science and technology, created modern civilization — 747s, antibiotics, the internet — from primate brains. This is not obvious; it is astonishing. If human intelligence did this, artificial general intelligence could do it again, faster, at scale.

**The model:** Intelligence is not just a cognitive property — it is a force of nature that reshapes the physical world. Understanding this reframes the stakes of AGI development.

**Application:** When evaluating the importance of AI research, don't compare it to other technologies. Compare it to the invention of science itself.

**Key quote:** "Human intelligence has created modern civilisation around us. Sometimes when I'm flying over to the US on a 747, I sometimes look out the window and think, 'how have we as humanity managed this with our sort of primate brains?' It seems incredible to me... So really the power of intelligence, human intelligence, has created the wonders of modern civilisation."

## Mental Model 4 — Biology as Computation

**Source:** Nobel lecture, Die Zeit, Nobel interview

Biology is not chemistry with extra steps — it is computation implemented in wet chemistry. Cells are computers. Proteins are programs. The genome is source code. This reframing makes AI the natural tool for biology.

**The model:** Once you see biology as information processing, the question shifts from "can AI help with biology?" to "what other tool could possibly work?"

**Application:** When approaching a biological problem, ask: what is the input? What is the computation? What is the output? This reframing often reveals the right AI architecture.

**Key quote:** "I think I've thought of biology as an information processing system at a fundamental level. Biology is a hugely complex, emergent dynamic system... I think AI systems and AI algorithms are quite a good match, a good description language, for biology."

---

## Mental Model 5 — The Timing Calibration

**Source:** Nobel interview

Hassabis's first games company failed because it was "20 years ahead of its time." He learned that ambition must be calibrated to the enabling conditions: data availability, compute, methods, and societal readiness.

**The model:** For any ambitious project, map the enabling conditions and their current state. If multiple conditions are not yet met, the project will fail regardless of the idea's quality. Monitor the conditions, not just the idea.

**Application:** Maintain a "readiness map" for your most ambitious projects. When conditions ripen, move fast. When they haven't, invest in the conditions rather than forcing the project.

**Key quote:** "Timing is very important. You need to pick hard problems to solve and be ambitious with that, but you've also got to pick the right time when the world and the context that you are in is the right kind of environment for those ideas to flourish."

---

## Mental Model 6 — The Contrastive AI-Brain Mechanism

**Source:** Sequoia/HTX interview, Die Zeit

AI systems provide a "contrastive mechanism" for understanding intelligence itself. By building artificial intelligence and comparing it to biological intelligence, we can study both systems in a controlled way — almost like a control experiment for consciousness and cognition.

**The model:** AI is not just a tool for science — it is a mirror for understanding the mind. The differences between AI and human cognition reveal what is essential about intelligence.

**Application:** When studying AI failures or unexpected behaviors, ask: what does this tell us about human cognition? The gap between AI and human performance is a research question, not just an engineering problem.

**Key quote:** "It provides a contrastive mechanism, allowing us to study and compare two different systems in depth, almost like a control experiment."
