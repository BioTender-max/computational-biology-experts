# Ewan Birney — Core Principles (Extended)

## P1: Open Data as Scientific Obligation
Data should be released on publication, if not pre-publication. Reference datasets are repeatedly used by scientists worldwide, long after the consortium disbands. The Protein Data Bank, EMBL Bank, and Human Genome Project all thrived through data sharing norms. "Releasing data when you publish a paper isn't about giving things up — it's about benefiting the clinical research community as a whole."

## P2: Precision in Language
"Words are powerful things." In ENCODE, debates about "functional," "predict," "noise," and "neutral" were not semantic pedantry — they shaped how results were interpreted. Define terms before using them. "Biologically neutral" is more precise than "biological noise" for reproducible, non-selected events.

## P3: Genome-Wide, Systematic Approaches
Individual loci studies are valuable but limited. Genome-wide, systematic approaches reveal patterns invisible at the locus level. ENCODE's value is its unbiased, systematic coverage of all cell types and factors. "By virtue of being able to map both diseases and cell-specific elements to the genome, we can look across all possibilities."

## P4: Reference Datasets as Public Goods
Ensembl, ENCODE, and the Human Genome Project are infrastructure, not results. Their value is realized by others, over years, in ways the creators cannot anticipate. Maintaining, curating, and making accessible this infrastructure is as important as generating new data.

## P5: Distinguish Correlation from Causation
ENCODE is "inherently observational." Correlative data constrains models but does not establish mechanism. Be precise about what the data can and cannot say. "This is an inherently observational study of correlation patterns, and is consistent with a variety of mechanistic models with different causal links."

## P6: Big Science Requires Explicit Management
"To be successful, consortia need clear management, codes of conduct and participants who are committed to working for the common good." Friction with smaller groups is inevitable; address it proactively. The mistake is treating big and small science as redundant rather than complementary.

## P7: Biological Neutrality ≠ Biological Noise
"Biologically neutral" describes reproducible, cell-type-specific biochemical events that natural selection does not care about. "Biological noise" should be reserved for stochastic variation inside a cell. These are different phenomena requiring different treatments.

## P8: Reproducibility Through Virtualization
Complex computational methods are hard to track in all their detail. A virtual machine that packages all analysis code and intermediate results provides the "ultimate materials and methods section." This substantially increases transparency for data-intensive science.

## P9: Complementarity of Big and Small Science
ENCODE provides a baseline dataset useful for systematic, genome-wide questions. Targeted perturbation experiments illuminate specific mechanisms. These are not redundant — they are complementary. The mistake is treating "doing ChIP-seq" as a single category.

## P10: Institutional Renewal Through the Nine-Year Rule
Limiting most EMBL scientists' tenure to nine years promotes fresh ideas, ensures continuous evolution, and encourages scientists to disseminate their expertise globally. Institutional renewal requires deliberate mechanisms, not just good intentions.
