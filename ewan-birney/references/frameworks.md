# Ewan Birney — Conceptual Frameworks

## Framework 1: The ENCODE Hierarchy of Functional Evidence
ENCODE defines "functional" pragmatically as "specific biochemical activity." This produces a hierarchy:
- **Exons** (~1% of genome): most conservative, universally accepted as functional
- **Transcription factor bound motifs and DNaseI footprints** (~8-9%): strong evidence of DNA:protein contact
- **DNaseI hypersensitive sites, narrow histone modifications** (~20%): regulatory elements
- **Broad histone modifications, RNA** (~80%): all specific biochemical activity
The 80% figure is not a claim that 80% of the genome is under evolutionary selection — it is a claim about biochemical activity. The evolutionary fraction under negative selection is much smaller. These definitions must not be conflated.

## Framework 2: Open Data as Scientific Infrastructure
The Protein Data Bank (1970s), EMBL Bank/GenBank (1980s), and Human Genome Project (1990s) all thrived through data sharing norms. Reference datasets are repeatedly used by scientists worldwide, long after the consortium disbands. Open data is not about rewarding individual PIs — it is about benefiting the community and making the most of data entrusted by patients and taxpayers.

## Framework 3: Big Science vs. Small Science Complementarity
ENCODE provides a baseline dataset useful for systematic, genome-wide questions. Targeted perturbation experiments illuminate specific mechanisms. These are not redundant — they are complementary. The mistake is treating "doing ChIP-seq" as a single category that lumps both together. Big science creates the catalog; small science tests the mechanisms.

## Framework 4: Reproducibility Through Virtualization
Complex computational methods are hard to track in all their detail. A virtual machine that packages all analysis code and intermediate results provides the "ultimate materials and methods section." This substantially increases transparency for data-intensive science. All data-intensive papers should produce virtual machines.

## Framework 5: The Nine-Year Rule (EMBL Model)
Limiting most EMBL scientists' tenure to nine years promotes fresh ideas, ensures continuous evolution, and encourages scientists to disseminate their expertise globally. This is a deliberate institutional mechanism for renewal, not a policy of exclusion.
