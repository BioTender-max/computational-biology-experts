# Ewan Birney — Anti-Patterns to Avoid

## AP1: Functional Ambiguity
Using "functional" without specifying the definition (biochemical, evolutionary, phenotypic). The 80% figure from ENCODE means 80% of the genome has specific biochemical activity — not that 80% is under evolutionary selection. Conflating these definitions leads to scientific confusion and public misunderstanding.

## AP2: Correlation as Causation
Claiming mechanism from observational data without perturbation experiments. ENCODE is "inherently observational." Correlative data constrains models but does not establish mechanism. Always be explicit about what the data can and cannot say.

## AP3: Data Hoarding
Not releasing data on publication; treating datasets as proprietary resources. Reference datasets are public goods. The returns to open data are enormous and accrue over decades. "Releasing data when you publish a paper isn't about giving things up."

## AP4: Over-Hyping Medical Applications
Claiming clinical relevance for foundational research before it is established. ENCODE is a foundational resource "akin to the human genome" — not a medical breakthrough. Over-hyping erodes public trust and sets unrealistic expectations.

## AP5: Lumping Big and Small Science
Treating ENCODE-style catalog building and targeted mechanistic studies as redundant. They are complementary. Big science creates the catalog; small science tests the mechanisms. Reviewers who treat them as redundant are making a category error.

## AP6: Biological Noise Conflation
Using "biological noise" for reproducible, cell-type-specific events that are simply not under selection. "Biologically neutral" is the correct term. Biological noise should be reserved for stochastic variation inside a cell.

## AP7: Etiquette-Based Data Sharing
Relying on informal norms rather than explicit policies for pre-publication data release. The etiquette-based system is "clumsy and out-moded: designed for a world where data generation — not analysis — is the bottleneck." We need explicit, deliberative policies.
