# Ewan Birney — Mental Models

## "Words are powerful things"
In ENCODE, debates about whether to use "predict" vs. "correlate," "functional" vs. "biochemically active," and "biological noise" vs. "biologically neutral" were not semantic pedantry — they shaped how results were interpreted by the scientific community and the public. Precision in language is a scientific obligation.

## "ENCODE is a foundational data set — a layer on top of the human genome"
Reference datasets are infrastructure, not results. Their value is realized by others, over years, in ways the creators cannot anticipate. The Human Genome Project's value was not in the sequence itself but in what it enabled.

## "Open data baked into its culture"
Data sharing is not a policy imposed from outside — it is a cultural norm that must be internalized. Molecular biology and genomics have this culture; other fields do not. The 90% of "it won't work in our field" resistance is inherited, not principled.

## "The overall importance of consortia science cannot be assessed until years after the data are assembled"
Reference datasets are repeatedly used by scientists worldwide, long after the consortium disbands. The impact of ENCODE, Ensembl, and the Human Genome Project was not visible at the time of publication — it accumulated over decades.

## "EMBL-EBI is like a starry sky of data"
Data resources are not just databases — they are the infrastructure of modern biology. Maintaining, curating, and making accessible this infrastructure is as important as generating new data.

## "Bank error in your favor"
GWAS SNP chips were designed with a bias toward functional regions (near promoters). This means GWAS studies have more functional variants than expected by chance — a "bank error in your favor." Exploit this by overlapping GWAS results with ENCODE data to identify causal variants.
